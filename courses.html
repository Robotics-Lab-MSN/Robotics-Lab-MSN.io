Courses & training

Courses and training in the lab

Graduate coursework and hands-on industry training that use the lab's robot cells, PLC workbenches, and simulation workstations.

Coursework in progress in the lab.

Taught in the lab

Graduate course

RAS 545 - Robotic Systems I

A core course of ASU's Robotics and Autonomous Systems master's program, taught by Prof. Sangram Redkar. Its guided laboratory series - articulated robot programming on the lab's Dobot arms and physics-based simulation in NVIDIA Isaac Sim - lives on this site.

See the instructional labs ↓

Undergraduate course

RAS 101 - Foundations of Robotic Engineering I

First course of ASU's undergraduate robotics sequence: robotic engineering as a profession, critical thinking in robot design and modeling, team dynamics, and engineering communication. Taught in this lab.

Undergraduate course

RAS 102 - Foundations of Robotic Engineering II

Second course of the undergraduate sequence: developing and experimentally verifying robotic engineering models, engineering design, data acquisition and analysis, and an introduction to CADD. Taught in this lab.

Industry training

PLC and automation training

Hands-on programmable logic controller and industrial automation training delivered by the lab's teaching team for industry partners, using the lab's Allen-Bradley PLC workbenches and robot cells.

From the RAS and manufacturing curriculum

The lab sits in the School of Manufacturing Systems and Networks, whose Robotics and Autonomous Systems and manufacturing programs include these related courses:

Course work in progress: students with a small quadcopter drone.

  • RAS 545 - Robotic Systems I: the program's robotics core, taught by Dr. Sangram Redkar - analysis and design of robotic systems: kinematics, dynamics, coordinate transformations and modeling. Its hands-on lab series lives on this site.
  • RAS 546 - Robotic Systems II: design of robotic systems, focusing on dynamics, modeling and controlling a robot.
  • RAS 550 - Mechatronic Systems: a systems approach to mechatronics - sensors, actuators, microcontrollers, and the everyday products and devices built on them.
  • RAS 557 - Foldable Robotics: design, simulate, analyze and prototype low-cost robotic devices using digital fabrication techniques such as laser cutting and Python.
  • RAS 585 - Machine Learning and AI: graduate course on machine learning and AI.
  • RAS 598 - Industrial Robotics and Smart Factory Automation: graduate special-topics course; recent student work includes the multi-robot wafer-handling cell built in this lab (see the Media page).
  • MFG 388 - Industrial Robotics: undergraduate course on industrial robotic systems - classification, control, programming and simulation, and deploying an industrial robot system.
  • MFG 524 - Engineering Computing with Python: solving engineering problems in Python - data structures, file I/O, and numerical computing with NumPy and SciPy through design, manufacturing and robotics problems.
  • MFG 598 - Programming of Industrial Robotics System: graduate special-topics course on programming industrial robotic systems.
  • MFG 598 - Additive Manufacturing: graduate special-topics course on additive manufacturing.
  • MFG 598 - CNC Computer Programming: graduate special-topics course on CNC computer programming.

The university's Robotics and Autonomous Systems courses page has the full program structure and elective lists.
